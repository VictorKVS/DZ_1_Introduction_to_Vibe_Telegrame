# Competitive Landscape & Technology Horizons — Strategic Bookmark v0.1

Status: ACTIVE BOOKMARK / CONTINUOUS REVIEW
Owner: Strategy + Technology Intelligence + Architecture Governance

## Purpose

Maintain a permanent strategic slice of competitors, adjacent platforms, enabling technologies and possible future horizons for FATHER.

This is not a one-time competitor table. It is a continuously reviewed map of the 'big game': where other players are, which capabilities are already proven, which business models work, which technologies may unlock new FATHER capabilities, and what conditions must exist before a horizon becomes economically or technically reachable.

The map is used for three purposes:

1. Strategic orientation — what capabilities define stronger players and where FATHER may differentiate.
2. Technology scouting — what existing products, OSS, standards, platforms and methods should be reused instead of rebuilt.
3. Horizon planning — what FATHER may become if specific technical, financial, data, legal or organizational conditions are achieved.

## 3. Capability comparison model

Do not ask only 'who is similar to FATHER?'. Decompose each system into organs/capabilities.

CAPABILITY → WHO HAS IT → MATURITY → BUSINESS PROOF → TECHNICAL APPROACH → CAN REUSE/INTEGRATE? → GAP FOR FATHER.

Examples of capabilities:
- ontology / enterprise graph;
- requirements-to-code traceability;
- spec-driven development;
- autonomous coding;
- agent orchestration;
- workflow execution;
- digital twin;
- process mining;
- DevSecOps automation;
- test generation and execution;
- runtime trace-to-design comparison;
- knowledge provenance;
- decision rationale/ADR;
- cost optimization;
- compliance automation;
- marketplace/ecosystem;
- cross-domain production.

A competitor can be strategically important even if only one of its organs is relevant.

## 4. Business-model slice

For each important product/platform analyze:

- what the customer actually buys;
- customer segment;
- land-and-expand mechanism;
- subscription / seat / consumption / transaction / platform / services pricing;
- cloud vs on-prem economics;
- consulting/professional services role;
- marketplace/revenue share;
- ecosystem incentives;
- data/network effects;
- switching costs;
- partner strategy;
- gross-margin implications where public/reasonably inferable;
- what asset becomes more valuable with every customer/project;
- what part may subsidize another part of the platform.

The goal is not imitation. The goal is to understand economically proven mechanisms that could support FATHER.

## 5. Technology Horizon Registry

A horizon is a capability that is not necessarily economical or mature today but may become reachable under defined conditions.

Each HorizonRecord contains:
- Horizon ID;
- capability/vision;
- current readiness level;
- enabling technologies;
- dependencies;
- required data/knowledge maturity;
- required compute/infrastructure;
- required legal/regulatory conditions;
- required human expertise;
- estimated cost threshold;
- expected value if achieved;
- major risks;
- precursor signals;
- competitor/player signals;
- trigger conditions;
- confidence;
- target review date.

Statuses:
WATCH → EXPERIMENT → CONDITIONAL → READY → ADOPT / REJECT / DEFER.

## 7. Unlock conditions

Every horizon must state what has to become true before we pursue it.

Example:
Horizon: FULL autonomous test+development loop.
Unlock conditions may include:
- traceability coverage > target;
- reliable requirements baseline;
- pre-code tests for critical flows;
- CI sandbox available;
- reproducible builds;
- agent benchmark reaches target pass rate;
- security controls embedded;
- cost per successful work package below human alternative;
- human intervention rate below threshold.

Until conditions are met the horizon remains WATCH/EXPERIMENT, not roadmap fiction.

## 8. Strategic distance / game levels

For visual planning, classify capabilities by distance:

NOW — available and economically usable with current resources.
NEXT — achievable after several concrete prerequisites.
LATER — plausible but depends on maturity/cost/data changes.
FRONTIER — technically imaginable/researched, high uncertainty.

The board should show both competitor position and FATHER position for each major capability.

Example format:
Capability | FATHER | Best observed player | Gap | Distance | Unlock condition | Reuse opportunity.

## 9. Build / Buy / Integrate / Learn

Every competitor/technology review should produce one or more actions:

BUILD — strategically differentiating capability we should own.
BUY — commodity capability better purchased.
INTEGRATE — strong external platform/OSS should become a module.
LEARN — copy the principle/business mechanism, not the code/product.
WATCH — no action yet; maintain signal collection.
AVOID — evidence suggests poor fit, unacceptable lock-in/risk/cost.

Default principle: do not rebuild mature commodity infrastructure merely for ownership pride.

## 11. Review cadence

This bookmark is permanent and continuously updated.

Recommended layers:
- event-driven update for major competitor/technology events;
- monthly quick strategic delta;
- quarterly landscape review;
- annual deep strategic horizon review;
- immediate review when a FATHER roadmap decision depends on a tracked capability.

## 12. Required outputs from each review

A review must not end as a news digest. It should update:

- competitor capability matrix;
- business-model matrix;
- technology/component reuse candidates;
- Build/Buy/Integrate decisions;
- horizon readiness;
- unlock conditions;
- risks/threats;
- new sources/evidence;
- FATHER roadmap implications;
- experiments to run.

## 13. Strategic rule

Treat the market like a strategy game board, but do not confuse aspiration with capability.

For every desired 'next level' FATHER must know:
WHERE WE ARE → WHO ALREADY PROVED PART OF IT → WHAT WE CAN REUSE → WHAT GAP REMAINS → WHAT CONDITIONS UNLOCK IT → WHAT IT COSTS → WHY IT IS WORTH DOING → HOW WE WILL VERIFY THE RESULT.
